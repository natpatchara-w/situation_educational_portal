## 1. Project Summary

This project aims to restructure crisis information flow at SMA Negeri 1 Bayah so instructions during an earthquake and potential tsunami are more responsive, efficient, and directed. Volunteers/students support communication-audit research, school spatial observation, data collection through FGDs and interviews, independent evacuation SOP drafting, visual evacuation-route mapping, and preparation for SOP socialization/testing in the Grand Simulation event.

Key context for volunteers: SMAN 1 Bayah is located in coastal South Lebak, an area highly vulnerable to megathrust earthquakes and tsunamis. During the short tsunami-evacuation golden time, panic, instruction bottlenecks, and disinformation can disrupt evacuation. The SOP must explain actors, actions, communication flows, and action phases during shaking and after shaking.

## 4. Transportation and Travel Preparation

Recommended transport mode: coordinated group transport, such as an organizer vehicle, minibus, van, or car convoy. This work involves school observation, visual documentation, printed materials, and coordination with many parties, so volunteers should avoid traveling separately.

### Main Transport Checklist

- [ ] Volunteer meeting point is set and shared in the group.
- [ ] Gathering time and departure time are confirmed.
- [ ] Transport lead is assigned.
- [ ] Driver name, phone number, and vehicle plate number are saved.
- [ ] Passenger list is prepared and checked before departure/return.
- [ ] Main route to SMA Negeri 1 Bayah is checked.
- [ ] Backup route and travel-time estimate are checked.
- [ ] Parking/drop-off plan at the school is confirmed.
- [ ] Return plan or vehicle waiting plan is confirmed.
- [ ] Emergency cash for parking, fuel, tolls, meals, or local transport is prepared.

### Last-Mile and Travel Safety Checklist

- [ ] Volunteers move in pairs or groups in unfamiliar areas.
- [ ] If local motorcycle transport is needed, use only organizer-approved riders and wear a helmet.
- [ ] Live location/check-in messages are shared with the coordinator.
- [ ] Offline maps, school address, and local contacts are saved on phones.
- [ ] Travel after dark happens only when the route, driver, and coordinator approval are clear.
- [ ] Research items, cameras, documents, and measuring tools are carried in labeled bags.

## 7. Key Messages Volunteers Should Understand

- An evacuation SOP is not only an exit route; it is a communication system: who gives instructions, who forwards them, who checks, and who reports.
- During shaking, instructions must be simple, fast, and consistent.
- After shaking, communication flows must help school members move toward safe points without waiting for unclear information.
- The evacuation map must be visually easy for students, teachers, staff, and visitors to understand.
- Field data must be verified; do not rely only on old maps or assumed procedures.
- School members are preparedness actors, not merely rescue objects.

## 9. Preparation Timeline

- [ ] Confirm schedule, location, duration, and agenda with the Supervisor/GMLS/school.
- [ ] Confirm permission for observation, interviews, FGDs, recording, and documentation.
- [ ] Assign volunteer roles: field coordinator, school liaison, FGD moderator, interviewer, note-taker, mapper, documentation lead, logistics lead, safety focal point, timekeeper.
- [ ] Finalize informant and FGD participant lists.
- [ ] Prepare observation forms, interview guides, and note templates.
- [ ] Request early data from the Data & Technology team: map, room data, demographic data, safe assembly points.
- [ ] Prepare the equipment and transport needs list.

### 2-3 Days Before Fieldwork

- [ ] Reconfirm schedule with the school and GMLS.
- [ ] Reconfirm transport: driver, plate number, meeting point, departure time, route, and return plan.
- [ ] Print or save offline all working documents.
- [ ] Brief the team on school ethics, consent, data protection, and role division.
- [ ] Test recorder, camera, power bank, and storage space.
- [ ] Prepare folder templates for photos, audio, notes, and findings recap.
- [ ] Check weather and route conditions.

### Day Before Fieldwork

- [ ] Share final rundown, contacts, and passenger list.
- [ ] Charge all devices.
- [ ] Pack personal, research, documentation, and FGD materials.
- [ ] Save Supervisor, Data & Technology Assistant Supervisor, GMLS, school, driver, and emergency contacts.
- [ ] Prepare a short opening to introduce the project to informants.
- [ ] Sleep enough.

### Fieldwork Day at SMA Negeri 1 Bayah

- [ ] Check volunteer attendance before departure.
- [ ] Arrive early and report to the school/GMLS contact.
- [ ] Confirm areas that may be accessed and photographed.
- [ ] Hold a short internal briefing: agenda, roles, time limits, and emergency meeting point.
- [ ] Observe the map, evacuation routes, assembly points, and communication infrastructure.
- [ ] Record bell/siren/loudspeaker locations, audio reach, noisy areas, narrow areas, and potential bottlenecks.
- [ ] Conduct FGDs/interviews according to consent.
- [ ] Make sure questions do not blame the school's current procedures; use collaborative language.
- [ ] Back up photos, audio, and notes before leaving.
- [ ] Thank informants and the school.

### After Fieldwork

- [ ] Copy and back up all files to the shared folder.
- [ ] Rename files using the format date_location_activity_number.
- [ ] Write an initial findings summary: actors, instruction flow, risk points, SOP revision needs.
- [ ] Mark incomplete data or items needing clarification.
- [ ] Synchronize with the Data & Technology team.
- [ ] Draft the initial SOP and evacuation map.

## 12. Conduct and Ethics Guidelines

- [ ] Use polite, clear language and avoid blaming existing procedures.
- [ ] Ask permission before recording audio, taking photos, or quoting informants.
- [ ] Do not share internal school data, detailed maps, or informant information without approval.
- [ ] Avoid making promises on behalf of GMLS, UMN, BNPB, BPBD, PMI, sponsors, or the school without authorization.
- [ ] When interacting with students, keep professional boundaries and avoid uncomfortable questions.
- [ ] Photograph/video students only when school permission and documentation consent are clear.
- [ ] Do not disrupt teaching and learning activities.
- [ ] Leave the school clean and tidy.

## 13. Draft SOP Quality Checklist

- [ ] SOP distinguishes the during-shaking and after-shaking phases.
- [ ] SOP identifies actors: Principal, duty teachers, class teachers, security/grounds staff, OSIS, students, and other relevant parties.
- [ ] SOP explains each actor's actions.
- [ ] SOP explains instruction and reporting flows.
- [ ] SOP anticipates communication bottlenecks.
- [ ] SOP considers classrooms/areas that may have difficulty receiving audio instructions.
- [ ] SOP aligns with the school's actual spatial layout.
- [ ] Visual evacuation map is easy to read and not overcrowded.
- [ ] Safe assembly points have been verified with GMLS/Data & Technology team/school.
- [ ] Draft is publicly reviewed with school management and the GMLS team before approval.
- [ ] Public-review feedback is recorded, prioritized, and followed up.
